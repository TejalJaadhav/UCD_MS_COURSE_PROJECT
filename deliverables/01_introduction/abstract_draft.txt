This project investigated whether component-level assessment can make citation support more informative than an overall label alone. A Python pipeline decomposed claims, checked decomposition fidelity, retrieved source passages, and assigned supported, unsupported, or contradicted labels before aggregation. Six configurations were compared on 1,085 cleaned claim-source pairs. The pre-verification Qwen 2.5 7B component method achieved the highest macro-F1, 0.5378, with 67.19% accuracy; majority prediction achieved 72.53% accuracy. Reviewed component annotations enabled separate measurement of localization and analysis of numerical, temporal, entity, qualifier, scope, and unsupported-addition mismatches. An experimental verification stage identified trade-offs that informed the default configuration. Test reuse and small AI-assisted annotation samples limit generalization. The project contributes a working citation-assessment tool, reproducible analyses, and evidence that overall classification and localized correctness require separate evaluation.
